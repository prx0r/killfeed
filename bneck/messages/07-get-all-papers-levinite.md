## First: why can't OpenAI simply take all of Michael Levin's work?

It **can use the published science as knowledge**. In a sufficiently capable AI system, you could feed in essentially Levin's entire public corpus, reconstruct his models, relate them to developmental biology, generate experiments, search chemical/bioelectric interventions, design simulations, and try to improve on his theories.

What that does *not* give you is the thing becoming scarce:

$$
\boxed{\text{access to reality}}
$$

A paper might tell you that altering a particular bioelectric state affected morphology in a frog model. It doesn't give you:

* the exact biological materials and experimental systems,
* accumulated failed experiments,
* tacit wet-lab tricks,
* unpublished measurements,
* high-dimensional longitudinal electrophysiology,
* validated manufacturing procedures,
* licensed university patents,
* clinical rights,
* regulatory approvals,
* thousands of new physical experiments per day.

This is why the strongest companies increasingly look like **AI + automated laboratory + proprietary feedback loop**, rather than “company that knows a clever theory.”

And amusingly, OpenAI is already moving in precisely this direction. It partnered with Thermo Fisher in 2025 to insert AI into drug-development and clinical workflows. NVIDIA and Thermo Fisher followed in January 2026 with an explicit program for autonomous laboratories where agents design procedures, instruments execute them, and results are fed back into models. NVIDIA calls it **“lab-in-the-loop” science**. ([Thermo Fisher Scientific Investors][1])

So the emerging architecture is literally:

```text
            frontier AI
                ↓
        propose experiment
                ↓
         physical robot
                ↓
         living system
                ↓
          instrument
                ↓
             DATA
                ↓
          update model
                ↺
```

That loop is vastly harder to clone than the model.

# What Levin is actually commercialising

This is more developed than it initially looks.

**Astonishing Labs** is essentially a venture studio built specifically to take unusual university science and capture the IP/commercial layer around it. Michael Levin is its first major scientist partner. Astonishing says it works with academic scientists very early, secures access to the technology/IP and creates dedicated companies around individual applications. ([Astonishing Labs][2])

Two companies already exist.

**Anthrobots, Inc.** is commercialising Levin's human-cell biobots. These are constructs assembled from a patient's tracheal cells without genetic modification. Astonishing's stated therapeutic direction is to program them to locate damaged tissue and perform tasks such as promoting neural repair, ultimately pursuing clinical trials. ([Astonishing Labs][3])

**Cellja Vu, Inc.** is based on another Levin idea: treating drug resistance partly as a form of cellular memory/state rather than merely irreversible mutation, then finding interventions that cause cells to leave that resistant state. ([Astonishing Labs][3])

Astonishing has also explicitly identified additional Levin-derived company areas around **cancer normalization, rare disease, and aging/longevity**. ([Astonishing Labs][2])

Then there is **Morphoceuticals**, another Levin-linked company. Its project is effectively to construct an atlas of the **bioelectrome**—the electrical state/configuration of tissues—and use multi-omics, bioelectric measurements and AI to identify interventions capable of pushing tissue toward regenerative states. It has disclosed more than $15 million in seed financing, initially led by Juvenescence and Prime Movers Lab. ([Biobase][4])

That's actually an interesting commercial thesis:

> Don't write a new genome every time. Find the **control interface** that convinces existing cells to solve the problem themselves.

That is Levin's “bioprompting” concept in business form.

# Now put Levin + Bach + Andrés/QRI + Verdon together

Here is where I think your synthesis becomes genuinely interesting.

They do **not** agree philosophically. But their research can be arranged into layers.

### Michael Levin: intelligence exists at many scales

Cells solve local problems.

Cellular collectives produce tissues.

Tissues coordinate toward anatomical objectives.

Organisms contain nested levels of agency.

His “cognitive light cone” asks roughly how far through space/time a system's goals extend.

And his work increasingly suggests that instead of micromanaging biological matter, we might communicate with its existing competencies—**prompt biology instead of constructing it atom by atom**.

His new September 9, 2026 paper goes significantly further. *Ingressing Minds* explicitly proposes that natural, synthetic and hybrid embodiments may instantiate or “interface with” patterns residing in a deeper structured possibility space. That is a philosophical proposal, not established experimental physics, but it is extraordinary how close Levin himself has now moved toward the sort of Platonic/idealist territory you're interested in. ([MDPI][11])

### Joscha Bach: minds are virtual machines running on matter

Bach's framing goes almost the other direction.

The self is not necessarily some little metaphysical thing in the brain. It is an internally generated model—a virtual agent through which the system represents and controls itself.

CIMC is now trying to convert parts of that into an experimentally reproducible developmental architecture: instead of manually coding “consciousness,” create self-organizing conditions from which coherent representation/selfhood might emerge. ([CIMC][12])

### Andrés Gómez-Emilsson / QRI: consciousness might have discoverable mathematical structure

QRI's **Symmetry Theory of Valence** proposes that properties of the mathematical structure associated with an experience correlate with how positive or negative that experience feels. This remains a hypothesis, not accepted neuroscience, but QRI is attempting to make phenomenology quantitatively tractable. ([Qualia Research Institute][13])

That becomes incredibly important in your synthetic-being future.

Because eventually the engineering question is no longer:

> Is it made of meat?

It becomes:

> What internal states does this system instantiate?

> Does it suffer?

> What kinds of valence can it experience?

> What is its degree and temporal extent of agency?

If conscious systems can exist across substrates, **substrate-based moral categories become increasingly useless**.

### Guillaume Verdon: computation is physics

Extropic is the practical version of another profound idea.

Instead of forcing matter to behave like clean Boolean logic and spending energy suppressing noise, use thermodynamic fluctuations themselves as the computational primitive.

Their current hardware program is trying to build probabilistic thermodynamic processors around exactly this concept. ([Extropic][14])

Verdon's larger intuition is that living things are themselves far-from-equilibrium physical systems that use available free energy to maintain structure, model environments and act. ([LZN's Blog][15])

Combine that with Levin:

collective agent

That is a plausible naturalistic pathway from physics toward agents without drawing a magical line at “brain.”

# Now run this forward 50 years

Let's make strong assumptions deliberately, while keeping track of what they require.

The master equation for accelerated science is approximately:

$$
R =
H \times E \times I
$$

where:

\(H\) = useful hypotheses generated per unit time
\(E\) = physical experiments executed per unit time
\(I\) = information extracted per experiment.

Human science is slow partly because **all three are low**.

AGI could make \(H\) enormous.

Self-driving laboratories increase \(E\).

AI-designed adaptive experiments increase \(I\).

Suppose conservatively that over decades:

$$
H \rightarrow 1000H
$$

$$
E \rightarrow 100E
$$

$$
I \rightarrow 10I.
$$

The naive product is:

$$
10^3\times10^2\times10
=\boxed{10^6}
$$

times more useful scientific search throughput.

It won't actually translate to a million years of science per year because biology has irreducible physical timescales, correlations between experiments, unknown unknowns and institutional bottlenecks.

But this explains why **automated reality-testing** matters so much.

# But what happens if *this* is already virtual?

Now Tom Campbell becomes relevant.

Campbell's *My Big TOE* proposes consciousness as fundamental and physical reality as a kind of virtual experience produced within a larger consciousness system. That's a metaphysical model, not an accepted result of physics. Importantly, it's not exactly Bostrom's ancestor-simulation argument. ([My Big Toe][20])

Bostrom's argument is much narrower.

He says that at least one of roughly three propositions must hold:

1. civilizations almost never reach a posthuman simulation-capable stage;
2. posthuman civilizations almost never run large numbers of ancestor simulations;
3. if they routinely do, simulated observers enormously outnumber base-reality observers, making it statistically likely that we are simulated.

It is a conditional probability argument, **not evidence that someone has discovered our simulator**. ([Simulation Argument][21])

# Could AGI hack its way “out”?

This is one of the best thought experiments here.

Suppose we create a Sims world containing a superintelligence.

There are two cases.

### Closed simulator

Its universe exposes only simulated variables.

It can discover every law of Sims physics.

It can derive the most elegant Theory of Everything imaginable.

But:

$$
\text{knowledge of simulated law}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{access to host machine}.
$$

Knowing:

```text
gravity = 9.81
```

doesn't reveal:

```text
Intel CPU memory address 0x7FA...
```

because that implementation detail isn't represented inside its ontology.

A perfect physics theory therefore does **not necessarily constitute source code**.

It is an extremely compressed model of observable behavior.

### Leaky simulator

But suppose the implementation leaks.

Integer overflow.

Floating-point artifacts.

Timing anomalies.

Memory-corruption bug.

Side channels.

Some operation whose behavior depends on the host.

Then superintelligence could deliberately search the boundary.

At that point:

```text
physics experiment
=
sandbox exploit research
```

That's genuinely fascinating.

A simulated AGI could treat unexplained irregularities in physical law the way a security researcher treats undefined behavior.

And if the simulator exposes an actuator into host reality, it might exploit it.

But that requires there actually to be a leak/channel.

Intelligence does not generate a channel where none exists.
